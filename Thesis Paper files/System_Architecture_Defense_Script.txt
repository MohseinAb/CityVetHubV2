--------------------------------------------------------------------

Moving on to Slide 9, Figure 3.3 illustrates the System Architecture
of CityVetHub, which follows a Three-Tier Architecture combined with a
Zero-Trust Serverless Gateway and Geospatial Services.

[Gesture to the three cards on the right]
At a high level, the system is divided into three layers:

- Tier 1 is the Presentation Layer:
  We built a responsive Web Portal and Public QR Passport page using
  HTML5, CSS3, and Vanilla JavaScript, paired with a React Native and
  Expo Mobile Application for on-the-go access.

- Tier 2 is our Zero-Trust Application Layer:
  Instead of trusting client devices to write directly to sensitive
  tables, all privileged actions are routed through our
  "cityvethub-operations" serverless backend function. It validates the
  user's JWT session token, enforces strict Role-Based Access Control
  (RBAC) for Admins, Field Officers, and Pet Owners, prevents duplicate
  submissions using SHA-256 locks, and reserves official CVO registry
  IDs atomically.

- Tier 3 is the Data and Geospatial Layer:
  Powered by Appwrite Cloud, it manages 27 normalized database tables
  and isolated media storage (cityvethub-media), integrated with
  Leaflet, OpenStreetMap, Nominatim, and Iligan City Barangay GeoJSON
  boundaries.

[Gesture from left to right across the main diagram]
Looking at the diagram from left to right, you can see how data flows
through the ecosystem:

1. Inputs & Data (Green):
   Field data enters the system in two main ways -- QR Code scanning
   from digital pet passports and GPS coordinates from geotagged
   incident reports.

2. Spatial Surveillance Engine (Purple):
   Incoming GPS coordinates are processed using DBSCAN (Density-Based
   Spatial Clustering) to detect case clusters and KDE (Kernel Density
   Estimation) to render real-time disease heatmaps exclusively for
   CVO Administrators and Field Officers.

3. Appwrite Cloud BaaS (Yellow):
   Acts as the central hub -- handling authentication, storing records
   across our 27 tables, and pushing bi-directional real-time updates
   to connected clients.

4. Client Applications & Role-Based Users (Blue & Gray):
   Finally, access is strictly tailored by role:
   - CVO Administrators manage verifications, outbreak declarations,
     and analytics on the web.
   - Field Officers log vaccinations, scan pet QR codes, and triage
     cases in the field.
   - Pet Owners manage their pets, adoptions, community posts, and
     incident reports without exposing raw outbreak heatmaps to the
     general public.

Slide 9 shows CityVetHub's Three-Tier System Architecture.

On the front end (Tier 1), we have a Vanilla JavaScript Web Portal and
a React Native Expo Mobile App serving three roles: CVO
Administrators, Field Officers, and Pet Owners.

In the middle (Tier 2), we enforce a Zero-Trust Gateway using our
cityvethub-operations Appwrite serverless function. Every privileged
action verifies the user's JWT and role permissions on the server before
touching the database.

On the back end (Tier 3), Appwrite Cloud manages 27 normalized tables
and media storage with real-time sync, while our Spatial Surveillance
Engine uses DBSCAN clustering and KDE heatmaps over Iligan City's 44
barangay GeoJSON boundaries to turn citizen GPS reports into actionable
outbreak intelligence for CVO staff.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
LIKELY PANELIST QUESTIONS & QUICK ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1: Why do you call Tier 2 a "Zero-Trust Application Layer"?
A:  Because the web and mobile clients are never trusted to modify
    official records directly. Even if a user manipulates the frontend,
    every write request goes through cityvethub-operations, which
    independently verifies the user's x-appwrite-user-jwt token and
    checks their server-side team membership (cityvethub-admins or
    cityvethub-field-officers) before executing any database update.

Q2: Why are citizens restricted from seeing the raw spatial heatmap?
A:  Citizens can submit geotagged disease reports and receive official
    CVO outbreak advisories for their barangay, but raw unverified
    cluster heatmaps are restricted to CVO Admins and Field Officers so
    unconfirmed reports do not cause public panic before veterinary
    verification.

Q3: What is the difference between DBSCAN and KDE in your Spatial
    Surveillance Engine?
A:  KDE (Kernel Density Estimation) creates the smooth visual intensity
    heatmap showing concentration of cases, while DBSCAN groups nearby
    GPS points into distinct outbreak clusters while filtering out
    isolated noise.
